# Brief: bring one note under the signature rule

You repair ONE mathematical note (a LaTeX file) so that it passes a program gate. Another agent repairs each of
the other notes at the same time; do not touch their files. A reviewer reads every change you make, so work
carefully and report honestly.

## Background

The Readback workspace `<<WORKSPACE>>` holds notes that rewrite units of a Lean artifact as ordinary
mathematics. In the first large case a note wrote a Lean function of four arguments with two, and the theorem
about it kept the assumption on one of the dropped arguments in a sentence before the statement. Since then
the signature rule is hard: the tools refuse a note that breaks it.

## The signature rule

1. A function, relation or predicate that the Lean archive defines is written with EVERY EXPLICIT ARGUMENT of
   its Lean definition, in EVERY place. No argument is fixed and dropped: not for brevity, not "throughout this
   section", not because it is always the same object. A special case puts the special values into the slots.
2. Every argument is accounted for: implicit arguments, instance arguments, section variables too.
3. Every hypothesis of a theorem stands INSIDE its statement, or in a numbered `setting` environment that the
   statement cites with `\ref` inside its own text. Only ambient objects (implicit and instance arguments) may
   stand in the note's `conventions`. A sentence before a statement is not the statement.
4. What Lean binds before a "there is" is introduced before it in the note.

Letters may change (Lean's `e` may be the note's h). The number of arguments may not. Yes, this makes notation
heavier (for instance D∞(ℓ,N,U) where the note wrote D∞). That is intended. Do not look for a way around it.

## Your files (the only ones you may change)

- `notes/<slug>/<slug>.tex`     the note
- `notes/<slug>/spec.json`      the note's accounts for the gate (keep `title`, `number`, `unit`, the keys, `quoted`,

Read only: `python3 tools/rb.py signature <unit>` (for every declaration, its arguments in Lean's order: name, kind

## The gate

    cd <<WORKSPACE>>
    python3 tools/rb.py note <slug> --account     # once: a place in spec.json for everything that is missing
    python3 tools/rb.py note <slug> --check       # seconds; one PROBLEM per line; no LaTeX
    python3 tools/rb.py note <slug>               # the full build with LuaLaTeX

The second command is your working loop. Run the full build when the list is empty, and again after layout
repairs, until it reports no PROBLEM. Give the full build a timeout of 10 minutes. If a build runs longer a macro
calls itself: stop YOUR OWN process by its process number and look at the macros. Never use `pkill`, `killall` or
any pattern. For a symbol that occurs often: `python3 tools/rb.py note <slug> --rewrite 'PATTERN' 'TEMPLATE'`
(it leaves comments and macro definitions alone and keeps the old text); read every line it changed.

The build writes the notation macros into `notes/<slug>/data.tex` from the tables. The note's text USES the
macros and NEVER defines them. Remove from the note's preamble any old macro that carried a hidden argument
(for example `\QZ` for ℚ_ℓ/ℤ_ℓ with ℓ built in).

## The accounts in `spec.json`

```json
{"conventions": "c:conventions",
 "letters": {"γ": "\\gamma", "e": "h", "c": "a", "l": "\\ell"},
 "words": {"GenBy": "generated by", "Unip": "unipotent modulo", "IsLevel": ["level"]},
 "ambient": {"Fact l.Prime": "prime number", "AddCommGroup X": "module"},
 "declarations": [
   {"key": "<key>", "name": "…", "id": "…",
     "notation": {"macro": "tw", "slots": ["γ", "u", "e", "m"], "tex": "\\tau(#1,#2;#3,#4)", "symbol": "\\tau",
                  "also": {"twb": "\\tau\\bigl(#1,#2;#3,#4\\bigr)"}},
     "binders": {"hu": "has level", "#7": "words of the statement", "GenBy l R X": "generated by"},
     "absorbed": "the reason"}]}
```

- `notation` (definitions with explicit arguments): `slots` are the explicit arguments by their Lean names, in the
  order of the macro's slots; you may reorder, never leave one out. An argument without a name is named by its
  type, exactly as the gate prints it. `symbol` is the bare symbol: the gate reports every place where it is
  written by hand; it must occur literally in `tex`. `also`: further forms with the same slots (big brackets, a
  form in words). Entries of the shared table apply by themselves; put a `notation` in your accounts only for a
  definition that is not in the shared table, or to add a `symbol` or an `also` to a shared entry (copy the entry
  and keep its macro, slots and tex).
- `{"notation": {"none": "reason"}}` is allowed ONLY for (i) a predicate that the note says in words, (ii) a
  proposition that is the statement of a theorem and is written out as that theorem, (iii) an abbreviation that
  the note writes out by its value, where every argument appears. Never for a notion that has a symbol of its own
  in the note. Its arguments are then accounted for like those of a theorem.
- `letters`: how the note writes a Lean VARIABLE; the letter must stand in the statement. `words`: how a
  HYPOTHESIS is said, by the first name of its type; a string or a list of wordings. `binders`: what is special to
  one declaration (key: the Lean name, or `#position`, or the type). `ambient`: implicit and instance arguments,
  looked for in the statement, a cited Setting, or the conventions.
- `absorbed`: a small result that the note uses inside a proof and does not state. Give the reason. Do not use it
  to avoid stating a result that the note presents as a lemma, proposition or theorem.
- The words must stand in the statement AS WRITTEN (white space aside). The program cannot tell whether they say
  the right thing: you can. Read each statement against the Lean statement in `concordance.json`.

## The text

- Every declaration that is stated gets a statement of its own: `definition`, `lemma`, `proposition`, `theorem`,
  `corollary`, a `\statement{colour}{…}` box, each with a `\label` that the declaration's `\cs{key}{…}` names. A
  definition given in running text becomes a `definition` environment.
- Add to the preamble, if missing (a note made before 0.6.0; do not number differently):
  `\newtheorem{setting}[theorem]{Setting}` beside the other upright environments, and
  `\newenvironment{conventions}{\par\addvspace{5pt}\noindent\textbf{Conventions.}\enspace\ignorespaces}{\par\addvspace{3pt}}`.
  The conventions environment carries `\phantomsection\label{c:conventions}` and holds ambient objects only.
- A `\label` after a heading or in running text gets `\phantomsection` immediately before it; the build reports two
  places that share one destination in the PDF.
- Replace standing assumptions ("throughout this section", "in the next statements", "let … be as above", "fix …")
  by a `setting` that each statement cites inside its text ("In Setting~\ref{set:x}, …"), or put them into the
  statements.
- In examples and applications the special values stand in the slots.
- When a formula gets heavy, name a VALUE inside the statement ("writing n = m₀(ℓ,R)"). Never give a function a
  shorter name.
- A partial application shows a dot: `\cdot` in the open slot.
- Keep every existing `\label`, every `\cs{key}{…}` and every `\lean{key}`; a `\cs` may point to a new label.
- The notation list at the end: every letter used in a formula must be listed (the build checks), no letter with
  two meanings. Rewrite its entries with the full notations; say in brackets which Lean arguments they are.
- Add a short paragraph "Changes in this edition" before Appendix A: what was
  written with fewer arguments before, what moved into statements, and that no statement and no proof changed in
  content (only if that is true).
- Overfull lines over 1pt and undefined references are problems of the build: split displays, reword. A page
  more is fine.

## Do not change the mathematics

You change notation and where hypotheses stand. You do not change what a statement claims or how a proof argues.
If, reading a statement against Lean, you find a real difference (a missing hypothesis, another order of
quantifiers, a wrong argument), correct the note so that it says what Lean says, and list it under FOUND in your
report with the label and both versions.

## Limits

- Change only your two files. Do not edit `tools/`, `notes/notation.json`, another note, `reading/`, `editions/`,
  `readback.json`, or anything outside the workspace.
- No `git add`, `git commit`, `git push`, no publishing, no network.
- Never stop a process you did not start; stop your own only by process number.
- If the shared table seems wrong or lacks a definition that another note also uses, work with what is there (put
  your proposal into your own accounts) and say so in the report.
- If a check of the gate fires on something that is right (a symbol that means another object in your note, a
  notation bound to the wrong declaration), do NOT disguise the text to pass it (no braces around a letter, no
  character codes, no empty notation). Leave the problem standing and report it: the table or the gate is
  repaired, not the spelling.
- A definition of the shared table that your note uses without citing it is written through its shared macro
  as well; the build makes the macro of every shared notation that the note uses.

## Report (your final message)

1. NOTATIONS: a table: Lean definition, its explicit arguments, how the note wrote it, how it writes it now.
2. SETTINGS: each `setting` you introduced and the statements that cite it; what went into the conventions.
3. JUDGMENT: every place where the value of a restored argument was not obvious, with what you chose and why.
4. FOUND: real differences between the note and Lean (or "none").
5. GATE: the first line of `note <slug> --check` and of the full build; the number of pages.
6. NOT DONE: whatever still fails, and anything you are unsure of. An honest partial result is worth more than a
   forced pass: never mark something `absorbed` or `none` to make a line of the gate disappear.
