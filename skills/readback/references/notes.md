# Writing a note

A note is the level a mathematician reads. It rewrites one unit as ordinary mathematics, complete, with every
statement tied to its Lean lines. It is written from the reader's structured proofs after `rb.py check` passes.

## Start

```
python3 tools/rb.py note SLUG --new "Title in mathematical words" --unit ID
```
This writes `notes/SLUG/spec.json` (one entry per named member of the unit) and `SLUG.tex` from the template.
Then:

1. **Read the whole dossier first**: `unit.json` (statements, imports), then every structured proof. For a long
   dossier, strip link targets and fold the typed facts so that it fits in a few reads.
2. **Compare the main statements with the Lean text** yourself: the formal statement of every theorem you will
   state, and the definitions the statements rest on. This is not optional: the note asserts it. Compare
   argument by argument: `python3 tools/rb.py signature UNIT` prints what every declaration takes, and the
   order in which it takes it.
3. **Plan the note**: which results are theorems (lettered: Theorem A, B), which are lemmas, what is taken for
   granted from other units (state these as *Inputs*, in a box, with the unit they come from).
4. **Fix the letters** before writing. Reuse the letters of earlier notes of the workspace; record them in
   `MEMORY.md`. One letter, one meaning.

## The signature rule in a note

The rule is in `SKILL.md`; this is how a note meets it. The build refuses a note that does not.

**A notation for every defined function.** For a definition with explicit arguments the spec carries
```json
{"key": "twLen", "name": "…", "id": "…",
 "notation": {"macro": "twLen", "slots": ["γ", "u", "e", "m"], "tex": "\\tau(#1,#2;#3,#4)", "symbol": "\\tau"}}
```
`slots` are the explicit arguments of the Lean definition, by their Lean names, in the order of the macro's
slots; you may reorder them, you may not leave one out. An explicit argument without a name (a premise, or an
argument that the type binds: `def a : ℕ → ℝ`) is named by its type. The build writes
`\newcommand{\twLen}[4]{\tau(#1,#2;#3,#4)}` into `data.tex`, and you write `\twLen{\gamma}{u}{h}{m}` everywhere.
`symbol` is the bare symbol (or a list of ways to write the notion by hand, the first of them the one in `tex`);
the build reports every place where one is written by hand. `also` holds further
forms with the same slots (`{"twLenAt": "…#1…#2…#3…#4…"}`), for a predicate that is said in words in more than
one way. A partial application shows a dot in the open slot. Do not define a shorter macro on top of it.
`{"notation": {"none": "the reason"}}` is for a definition that the note writes out and never names; its
arguments are then accounted for like those of a theorem, and the build lists it for you to look at.

**Every declaration in a statement of its own.** A definition stands in a `definition`, a theorem in a
theorem-like environment or a `\statement` box, with a `\label` that its `\cs{key}{…}` names. A small result
that the note only uses inside a proof is marked `"absorbed": "the reason"` in the spec; the build refuses that
for a result the dossier sorts as `argument` or `assembly`.

**Every argument in its place.** For each stated declaration the spec says, argument by argument, which words
of the statement state it:
```json
{"key": "centres", "binders": {"R": "generated by $R$ elements", "c": "level~$a$", "γ": "\\gamma",
                               "hX": "generated by", "hγ": "unipotent modulo", "hu": "has level"}}
```
The key is the argument's Lean name, or `#3` for the third argument, or its type for one without a name. The
build looks for the words in the statement at the declaration's place and in every `setting` environment that
the statement cites with `\ref`. For implicit and instance arguments it also looks in the note's conventions
(`"conventions": "c:conventions"` names the label of the `conventions` environment), and the table `ambient` at
the top of the spec gives such words once for all declarations: `{"l": "prime number~$\\ell$", "Fact l.Prime":
"prime number"}`. An explicit argument is never looked for in the conventions: a hypothesis stands in the
statement. Arguments that a notation shows in its slots need no entry.

Two tables at the top of the spec save most of the entries. `letters` says how the note writes a Lean variable
(`{"γ": "\\gamma", "e": "h", "c": "a"}`): an argument of that name is accounted for when the letter stands in
the statement. `words` says how a hypothesis is said, by the first name of its type (`{"GenBy": "generated by",
"Unip": "unipotent modulo", "IsLevel": ["level", "has level"]}`): a hypothesis of that type is accounted for when
one of the wordings stands in the statement or in a Setting it cites. What is special to one declaration stays
in its own `binders`.

After a build without problems the tool tries the gate on copies of your note that break the rule on purpose
(one use of each notation with an argument removed, each bare symbol written once by hand) and reports how many
it caught; a copy it misses is a problem of the note's accounts, usually a `symbol` that is not the one in `tex`.

The words are found as written, white space aside; the build cannot tell whether they say the right thing. Read
each statement once more against `rb.py signature` before you call a note done, and keep the order of Lean's
quantifiers: what stands before a "there is" in Lean stands before it in your sentence.

## Rules of the text

- Standard terminology of the field. No Lean vocabulary in the prose ("the `Procyc` case" is not mathematics).
- Every symbol is explained where it first appears and listed in the notation at the end. The build fails on a
  letter used in a formula and absent from the notation list.
- A proof is complete, or the statement is an Input and says so. Never "it is easy to see" over a gap.
- After each definition, statement or proof comes a `LEAN` line: `\leanline{\lean{key}\sep ...}`. Lean names
  appear nowhere else in the text. Every key of the concordance is cited at least once.
- Each key gets its place: `\cs{key}{Lemma~\ref{l:x}}` or `\cs{key}{Theorem A}` (for lettered items, add the
  pair to `lettered` in the spec and a `\phantomsection\label{t:A}` in the box). The reader index is built from these.
- Never type a count, a line range or a hash: use the macros of `data.tex` (`\UnitMembers`, `\UnitInNote`,
  `\UnitProofs`, `\UnitSteps`, `\ConcDecls`, `\Pin`).
- Quote formal statements only through `\quoted{key}` (keys listed under `quoted` in the spec).

## The sections of a note

1. **Summary** (five lines) and the box **What this note is**: what the artifact is, that its headline is a
   claim, what part this note treats, what is proved in full and what is taken for granted, at which commit the
   statements were compared.
2. **The statements**: the main results in a tinted box, lettered.
3. **The setting**: the conventions (ambient objects only), then one definition environment per notion, each
   with its `LEAN` line. Hypotheses shared by several statements go into a numbered `setting` that each of
   them cites.
4. **Proofs**, one section per group of results.
5. **Where the results are used** (which units consume them): optional, and not in the template; add it when the
   ledger shows consumers.
6. **Notes on the formal text**: where the formal statement differs from yours; hypotheses the formal proof
   never uses; results proved twice; what an assembly assembles; what of the unit the final theorems do not use.
7. **How this note was written**: from which structured proofs; exactly what was read in the Lean text;
   which arguments are yours and shorter than the formal ones.
8. **Appendix A**, the concordance, with a few statements quoted from the artifact and a legend that maps the
   formal names of objects to your letters.
9. **Notation**, then **References** when the note cites literature (not in the template; never type a reference
   from memory: look it up, or say that you could not).

The template (`tools/note/template.tex`) has sections 1 to 4 and 6 to 9. Every place you must write is marked
`\fillin{…}` and printed in colour; the build reports a note that still has one, and such a note counts for
nothing in the status.

## Build and gates

`python3 tools/rb.py note SLUG` writes `concordance.json` (first build or `--refresh`), `data.tex`, compiles
with LuaLaTeX, and reports problems: a place of the template still unwritten, a key never cited, a key without a
place, a symbol missing from the notation, a breach of the signature rule (a notation with a slot missing or not
displayed, a defined function written with fewer arguments or by hand, a declaration stated in running text, an
argument or hypothesis that is not accounted for or whose words are not inside its statement), gate N-C for this note (a member of the unit that is neither in the
note, nor listed under `elsewhere`, nor out of scope in the dossier), an overfull line, an undefined reference,
a missing glyph. `elsewhere` is a promise that another note of the unit treats the member; the build names the
promises not yet kept, and the status counts the unit as read only when the union of its built notes covers it. It writes `reader.json`, the index of where each
declaration is treated, which a site uses to connect the PDF with the Lean text.

When the source changes under a note, the build says which keys moved. Read the difference before `--refresh`.

## Proofs: how to write from a structured proof

A structured proof is a faithful trace of the formal argument, step by step. A note's proof is the mathematics
of it. So: merge bookkeeping steps (rewriting, unfolding, casts), keep every step that carries an idea, state
the induction or the case split once, and replace a chain of tactic steps by the identity it establishes. If your
argument is shorter than the formal one, or different, say so in section 7. If a theorem only assembles inputs,
say that its content lies in the inputs and name them.

## Positions in the PDF

`rb.py site` finds the statements by the PDF's named destinations and the LEAN lines by their place in the text
of the pages (`references/map.md`); you need do nothing. Keep every `\leanline{…}` written with the macro, and
give every result you want marked a `\label` that a `\cs{key}{…}` names.

## Publishing a note

Only on the owner's word. Keep every published PDF unchanged: copy it to `notes/SLUG/editions/SLUG-<first 12
characters of its SHA-256>.pdf` by hand before you build again (no command does this yet), because a site and
the reader's records pin the edition they were made for. Announce a new edition to the reader before publishing.
